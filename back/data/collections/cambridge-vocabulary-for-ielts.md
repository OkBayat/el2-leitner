# Cambridge Vocabulary for IELTS

## Unit 1 — Growing up

- adolescence
  - definition: The stage of life between childhood and adulthood.
  - example: Their friendship remained strong throughout adolescence.

- adulthood
  - definition: The period of life when a person is fully grown.
  - example: Stress in adulthood can sometimes be linked to childhood experiences.

- bond
  - definition: A strong emotional connection between people.
  - example: There is a close bond between the two brothers.

- brotherhood
  - definition: The state or relationship of being brothers or a close group.
  - example: Their shared experiences created a lasting brotherhood.

- character
  - definition: The qualities that make someone the kind of person they are.
  - example: Patience is an important part of her character.

- childhood
  - definition: The period of life when a person is a child.
  - example: He remembers his childhood as a happy time.

- conflict
  - definition: A serious disagreement or struggle between people or ideas.
  - example: Poor communication can create conflict in a family.

- connection
  - definition: A link or relationship between people or things.
  - example: She has a close connection with her grandmother.

- fatherhood
  - definition: The state or experience of being a father.
  - example: Fatherhood changed the way he thought about responsibility.

- friendship
  - definition: A close relationship between people who like and support each other.
  - example: Their friendship began when they were young children.

- instinct
  - definition: A natural feeling that makes you act without thinking first.
  - example: Her instinct was to protect the child immediately.

- interaction
  - definition: Communication or activity between people who affect each other.
  - example: Positive interaction helps children develop social skills.

- motherhood
  - definition: The state or experience of being a mother.
  - example: She found motherhood both demanding and rewarding.

- nature
  - definition: A person's basic character or natural way of behaving.
  - example: It is in his nature to help other people.

- parent
  - definition: A mother or father of a child.
  - example: Every parent develops their own way of caring for a child.

- relation
  - definition: A connection between two ideas, events, or situations.
  - example: The study looked at the relation between stress and family life.

- relationship
  - definition: The way two people or things are connected with each other.
  - example: She has a strong relationship with her parents.

- relative
  - definition: A person who is a member of your family.
  - example: An older relative helped him choose his first car.

- resemblance
  - definition: The fact that two people or things look or seem similar.
  - example: There is a clear resemblance between the sisters.

- rivalry
  - definition: Competition between people or groups who want the same thing.
  - example: Friendly rivalry can sometimes make people work harder.

- sibling
  - definition: A brother or sister.
  - example: A new sibling can change a young child's family life.

- teenager
  - definition: A person between thirteen and nineteen years old.
  - example: The teenager often asks friends for advice.

- temperament
  - definition: A person's natural emotional character and usual way of reacting.
  - example: She inherited her father's calm temperament.

- ties
  - definition: Strong connections between people, families, or groups.
  - example: Their family ties stayed strong after they moved apart.

- upbringing
  - definition: The way a child is cared for and taught while growing up.
  - example: He had a stable upbringing in a supportive family.

- active role
  - definition: An important part in an activity that involves direct involvement.
  - example: Both parents played an active role in school life.

- extended family
  - definition: Family members beyond parents, children, brothers, and sisters.
  - example: Our extended family includes many aunts, uncles, and cousins.

- family gathering
  - definition: An occasion when members of a family meet together.
  - example: The family gathering was noisy but enjoyable.

- immediate family
  - definition: Your closest family members, usually parents, children, and siblings.
  - example: My immediate family is quite small.

- maternal instinct
  - definition: A natural feeling to care for and protect children.
  - example: She says her maternal instinct makes her enjoy caring for babies.

- sibling rivalry
  - definition: Competition or jealousy between brothers and sisters.
  - example: There was little sibling rivalry in their home.

- stable upbringing
  - definition: A childhood with regular care, safety, and support.
  - example: A stable upbringing can help children feel secure.

- striking resemblance
  - definition: A very clear and noticeable similarity in appearance.
  - example: She bears a striking resemblance to her grandmother.

- close
  - definition: Having a strong and friendly relationship with someone.
  - example: The two sisters are very close.

- close-knit
  - definition: Having members who know, help, and support each other closely.
  - example: They grew up in a close-knit family.

- maternal
  - definition: Related to a mother or to the mother's side of a family.
  - example: She looks very much like her maternal grandmother.

- parental
  - definition: Related to parents or the care given by parents.
  - example: Young children need strong parental support.

- rewarding
  - definition: Giving satisfaction or a feeling that something was worthwhile.
  - example: Caring for children can be very rewarding.

- stable
  - definition: Firm, safe, and unlikely to change suddenly.
  - example: Children often benefit from a stable home environment.

- accommodate
  - definition: To change or arrange something to meet someone's needs.
  - example: Good parents try to accommodate a young child's needs.

- adopt
  - definition: To begin using a new method, idea, or way of doing something.
  - example: The company decided to adopt a new recruitment method.

- break down
  - definition: To stop working successfully, especially a relationship or system.
  - example: A relationship can break down when people stop communicating.

- develop
  - definition: To grow, change, or become more advanced over time.
  - example: Children develop important social skills through play.

- endure
  - definition: To continue for a long time despite difficulty or change.
  - example: Some childhood friendships endure for many years.

- establish
  - definition: To create something so that it becomes strong or accepted.
  - example: It takes time to establish a good working relationship.

- have something in common
  - definition: To share the same interests, experiences, or qualities.
  - example: My sister and I have something in common despite our differences.

- inherit
  - definition: To receive a physical quality or characteristic from your family.
  - example: He seemed to inherit his father's mathematical ability.

- interact
  - definition: To communicate or do things with another person or group.
  - example: Children learn a lot when they interact with other children.

- nurture
  - definition: To care for and encourage someone or something to develop.
  - example: Good teachers nurture the talents of their students.

- play a role
  - definition: To have a part or influence in a situation or activity.
  - example: Parents play a role in children's social development.

- relate to
  - definition: To understand and respond to someone or be connected with something.
  - example: Parents often relate to a child differently from friends.

## Unit 2 — Mental and physical development

- ability
  - definition: The skill or power to do something.
  - example: Children develop the ability to think more logically as they grow.

- adolescent
  - definition: A young person who is developing from a child into an adult.
  - example: Each adolescent develops at a slightly different rate.

- behaviour / behavior
  - definition: The way a person or animal acts.
  - example: Parents often notice changes in a teenager's behaviour.

- concept
  - definition: An idea, especially one that may be difficult to understand.
  - example: Young children can find an abstract concept difficult to understand.

- consequence
  - definition: A result that happens because of an action or event.
  - example: Teenagers learn to accept the consequence of their actions.

- gesture
  - definition: A movement of the hands, head, or body used to communicate.
  - example: A baby may use a simple gesture before learning many words.

- growth
  - definition: The process of becoming bigger, older, or more developed.
  - example: Childhood growth is rapid during some stages.

- height
  - definition: The measurement of how tall someone or something is.
  - example: Teenagers often gain a lot of height in a short time.

- imagination
  - definition: The ability to form pictures or ideas in your mind.
  - example: Make-believe games help a child use their imagination.

- infancy
  - definition: The earliest period of life when someone is a baby.
  - example: Physical development is extremely rapid during infancy.

- infant
  - definition: A baby or very young child.
  - example: The infant learned to sit without support.

- knowledge
  - definition: Information and understanding that a person has learned.
  - example: Children gain knowledge through study and experience.

- maturity
  - definition: The state of being fully developed physically or emotionally.
  - example: Emotional maturity often develops gradually.

- memory
  - definition: The ability to remember information, experiences, and events.
  - example: She uses visual images to improve her memory.

- milestone
  - definition: An important stage or achievement in development.
  - example: Learning to walk is a major milestone for a child.

- mind
  - definition: The part of a person that thinks, remembers, and feels.
  - example: Travel can broaden the mind and introduce new ideas.

- peers
  - definition: People of about the same age or social group.
  - example: Teenagers often compare themselves with their peers.

- period
  - definition: A length or stage of time.
  - example: Adolescence is a period of major change.

- phase
  - definition: A particular stage in a process of change or development.
  - example: Many children go through a clumsy phase.

- rate
  - definition: The speed at which something happens or changes.
  - example: Children do not all grow at the same rate.

- reminder
  - definition: Something that helps you remember a person, event, or task.
  - example: She wrote a note as a reminder to study.

- social skills
  - definition: Abilities that help you communicate and behave well with other people.
  - example: Playing with friends helps children develop social skills.

- skill
  - definition: An ability learned through practice or experience.
  - example: Reading is a skill that improves with practice.

- stage
  - definition: A particular point in a process of growth or development.
  - example: Middle childhood is an important stage of development.

- toddler
  - definition: A young child who has recently learned to walk.
  - example: The toddler can now run and climb stairs.

- transition
  - definition: The process of changing from one state or stage to another.
  - example: The transition from school to university can be stressful.

- abstract
  - definition: Based on ideas rather than physical objects or real examples.
  - example: Older students can discuss more abstract ideas.

- cognitive
  - definition: Related to thinking, learning, understanding, and remembering.
  - example: Problem solving is part of cognitive development.

- clumsy
  - definition: Moving awkwardly and often dropping or hitting things.
  - example: Teenagers can become clumsy during a sudden growth spurt.

- fond
  - definition: Having warm and happy feelings about someone or something.
  - example: She has fond memories of her school days.

- fully-grown / fully grown
  - definition: Having reached full physical size and development.
  - example: By adulthood the body is usually fully-grown.

- immature
  - definition: Not behaving or developing in a way expected for your age.
  - example: Some children seem immature compared with their classmates.

- independent
  - definition: Able to do things without needing much help from others.
  - example: A toddler slowly becomes more independent.

- irresponsible
  - definition: Not showing enough care about the effects of your actions.
  - example: Leaving a young child alone would be irresponsible.

- mature
  - definition: Fully developed or behaving in a sensible adult way.
  - example: Some teenagers are very mature for their age.

- patient
  - definition: Able to wait or deal with problems without becoming annoyed.
  - example: Parents need to be patient with young children.

- rebellious
  - definition: Unwilling to obey rules or accept control.
  - example: He became more rebellious during adolescence.

- significant
  - definition: Important or large enough to be noticed.
  - example: There is a significant increase in height during adolescence.

- tolerant
  - definition: Willing to accept behaviour or opinions that are different from your own.
  - example: Good parents try to be tolerant of small mistakes.

- acquire
  - definition: To gain or learn something over time.
  - example: Children acquire language skills through regular interaction.

- grow
  - definition: To become bigger, older, or more developed.
  - example: Children grow at different speeds.

- imitate
  - definition: To copy the actions, sounds, or behaviour of another person.
  - example: Babies often imitate the people around them.

- look back
  - definition: To think about something that happened in the past.
  - example: She likes to look back on her childhood holidays.

- master
  - definition: To learn a skill so well that you can do it confidently.
  - example: Children gradually master reading and writing.

- remember
  - definition: To keep or bring information or experiences back into your mind.
  - example: I still remember my first day at school.

- remind
  - definition: To make someone remember something they might forget.
  - example: This photograph can remind me of my childhood.

- reminisce
  - definition: To talk or think happily about past experiences.
  - example: The sisters often reminisce about old family holidays.

- throw a tantrum
  - definition: To suddenly cry, shout, or behave angrily, especially as a child.
  - example: A tired toddler may throw a tantrum in a shop.

- visualise / visualize
  - definition: To form a clear picture of something in your mind.
  - example: Some learners visualise a word to remember it better.

- typically
  - definition: In the way that usually happens or is normally expected.
  - example: Girls typically experience some growth changes earlier than boys.

- bear in mind
  - definition: To remember and consider something when making a decision.
  - example: Bear in mind that every child develops differently.

- broaden the mind
  - definition: To increase your knowledge and understanding of different ideas.
  - example: Reading widely can broaden the mind.

- have something in mind
  - definition: To have a particular idea or plan you are thinking about.
  - example: I have something in mind for my next study project.

- have something on your mind
  - definition: To be thinking or worrying about something a lot.
  - example: You may have something on your mind when you seem distracted.

- it slipped my mind
  - definition: Used to say that you forgot something.
  - example: I meant to call you, but it slipped my mind.

- keep an open mind
  - definition: To avoid deciding before you know all the facts.
  - example: Keep an open mind when you hear a new idea.

- my mind went blank
  - definition: Used when you suddenly cannot remember or think of anything.
  - example: During the test my mind went blank for a moment.

- put your mind at ease
  - definition: To make you stop worrying about something.
  - example: The doctor explained the results to put your mind at ease.

## Unit 3 — Keeping fit

- allergy
  - definition: A condition where the body reacts badly to a substance or food.
  - example: Her peanut allergy means she must check food labels carefully.

- anxiety
  - definition: A strong feeling of worry or nervousness.
  - example: Long periods of stress can increase anxiety.

- appetite
  - definition: The feeling that makes you want to eat.
  - example: Regular exercise can improve your appetite.

- artery
  - definition: A blood vessel that carries blood away from the heart.
  - example: A blocked artery can cause a serious health problem.

- asset
  - definition: Something useful or valuable that gives an advantage.
  - example: Some stress can be an asset during an important event.

- benefit
  - definition: A helpful or positive effect.
  - example: Regular exercise has an important health benefit.

- cravings
  - definition: Very strong desires for particular foods or substances.
  - example: Stress can cause cravings for salty or sugary food.

- depression
  - definition: A serious condition that causes long-lasting sadness and loss of interest.
  - example: Persistent stress can be linked to depression.

- diagnosis
  - definition: A doctor's decision about what illness or condition a person has.
  - example: The doctor made a diagnosis after several tests.

- diet
  - definition: The food and drink that a person usually consumes.
  - example: A balanced diet can reduce some health risks.

- dietician / dietitian
  - definition: A specialist who advises people about food and healthy eating.
  - example: The dietician recommended more fruit and vegetables.

- disease
  - definition: An illness that affects the body or mind.
  - example: Heart disease can be influenced by several risk factors.

- eating disorder
  - definition: A health condition involving harmful eating habits or attitudes to food.
  - example: An eating disorder often needs professional treatment.

- exercise
  - definition: Physical activity done to keep the body healthy and strong.
  - example: Regular exercise helps the heart work efficiently.

- factor
  - definition: One of the things that influences a result or situation.
  - example: Age is one factor that can affect a healthy weight.

- fast food
  - definition: Food prepared and served quickly, often high in fat or salt.
  - example: Eating too much fast food can lead to weight gain.

- fat
  - definition: A substance in food that provides energy but can be unhealthy in excess.
  - example: Some processed foods contain a lot of fat.

- harm
  - definition: Physical or mental damage.
  - example: Too much stress can cause harm over time.

- health
  - definition: The condition of a person's body and mind.
  - example: A good diet supports both physical and mental health.

- heart attack
  - definition: A serious event caused when blood flow to the heart is blocked.
  - example: A blocked artery can lead to a heart attack.

- infection
  - definition: An illness caused by harmful bacteria, viruses, or other organisms.
  - example: The doctor treated the infection with medicine.

- ingredients
  - definition: The foods or substances used to make a dish or product.
  - example: Check the ingredients before buying packaged food.

- insomnia
  - definition: A condition in which a person has difficulty sleeping.
  - example: Unresolved stress can lead to insomnia.

- intake
  - definition: The amount of food, drink, or another substance taken into the body.
  - example: Try to reduce your intake of salt and sugar.

- junk food
  - definition: Food with little nutritional value, often high in fat, salt, or sugar.
  - example: He eats too much junk food when he is busy.

- muscle
  - definition: Body tissue that can tighten and move parts of the body.
  - example: The heart is a muscle that pumps blood.

- nutrient
  - definition: A substance in food that the body needs to grow and stay healthy.
  - example: Iron is an important nutrient found in many foods.

- nutrition
  - definition: The process of getting and using the food needed for health.
  - example: Good nutrition is important for healthy development.

- obesity
  - definition: A health condition in which a person has too much body fat.
  - example: Obesity can increase the risk of several diseases.

- onset
  - definition: The beginning of an illness or problem.
  - example: Chronic stress may be linked to the onset of depression.

- portion
  - definition: An amount of food served to one person.
  - example: Try to eat a moderate portion at each meal.

- risk
  - definition: The possibility that something harmful may happen.
  - example: Smoking increases the risk of heart disease.

- serving
  - definition: A measured amount of food for one person.
  - example: One serving of fruit can be part of a healthy breakfast.

- stress
  - definition: Mental or physical pressure caused by difficult situations or demands.
  - example: Too much stress can affect sleep and appetite.

- stroke
  - definition: A serious condition caused when blood flow to part of the brain is blocked.
  - example: High blood pressure can increase the risk of stroke.

- treatment
  - definition: Medical care given to improve an illness or condition.
  - example: The patient received treatment immediately.

- therapy
  - definition: A method of treating a physical or mental health problem.
  - example: Relaxation therapy can help reduce stress.

- variety
  - definition: A range of different types of the same general thing.
  - example: A healthy diet should include a variety of foods.

- weight
  - definition: How heavy a person or thing is.
  - example: Regular exercise can help you control your weight.

- acute
  - definition: Severe and usually beginning suddenly or lasting a short time.
  - example: Acute stress can be harmful in some situations.

- allergic
  - definition: Having a physical reaction to a particular substance or food.
  - example: She is allergic to nuts.

- alternate
  - definition: Happening or used every other time in a regular pattern.
  - example: The trainer suggested exercise on alternate days.

- brisk
  - definition: Quick and energetic.
  - example: A brisk walk is a simple form of aerobic exercise.

- chronic
  - definition: Continuing for a long time or happening repeatedly.
  - example: Chronic pain can become worse when stress is high.

- harmful
  - definition: Causing damage or injury.
  - example: Too much saturated fat can be harmful to health.

- healthy
  - definition: In good physical condition or helping to produce good health.
  - example: A healthy diet includes fresh food and regular meals.

- infectious
  - definition: Able to spread from one person to another and cause illness.
  - example: The patient was isolated because the disease was infectious.

- moderate
  - definition: Neither very large nor very small; within a reasonable level.
  - example: Moderate exercise is suitable for many people.

- obese
  - definition: Having a level of body fat that is seriously above a healthy range.
  - example: Obese patients may face a higher risk of heart disease.

- overweight
  - definition: Heavier than the weight considered healthy for a person's height.
  - example: He became overweight after years of little exercise.

- persistent
  - definition: Continuing for a long time or refusing to go away.
  - example: Persistent stress can affect several parts of the body.

- regular
  - definition: Happening often and in a consistent pattern.
  - example: Regular exercise can improve cardiovascular health.

- vital
  - definition: Extremely important or necessary.
  - example: Fresh food provides vital nutrients.

- avoid
  - definition: To stay away from something or prevent yourself from doing it.
  - example: People with allergies must avoid certain foods.

- counteract
  - definition: To reduce or oppose the effect of something harmful.
  - example: Some people eat sugary food to counteract feelings of tension.

- curb
  - definition: To control or reduce something unwanted.
  - example: Eating regular meals can help curb your appetite.

- cure
  - definition: To make an illness or health problem go away completely.
  - example: There is no simple cure for every chronic condition.

- diminish
  - definition: To become or make something smaller, weaker, or less important.
  - example: Long-term stress can diminish quality of life.

- disrupt
  - definition: To interrupt the normal operation or pattern of something.
  - example: Stress hormones can disrupt normal sleep.

- eliminate
  - definition: To remove something completely.
  - example: You do not need to eliminate every favourite food from your diet.

- maintain
  - definition: To keep something at the same healthy or effective level.
  - example: Exercise can help you maintain a healthy weight.

- overdo
  - definition: To do too much of something.
  - example: Beginners should exercise carefully and not overdo it.

- overeat
  - definition: To eat more food than your body needs.
  - example: Skipping meals can make some people overeat later.

- prevent
  - definition: To stop something from happening.
  - example: A healthy lifestyle can help prevent some diseases.

- recommend
  - definition: To advise that something is good or suitable.
  - example: Doctors often recommend regular aerobic exercise.

- recover
  - definition: To become healthy or strong again after illness or effort.
  - example: Your body needs time to recover after hard exercise.

- reduce
  - definition: To make something smaller or less.
  - example: Walking regularly can reduce some health risks.

- skip
  - definition: To not do or have something that you normally would.
  - example: Try not to skip breakfast when you are busy.

- stimulate
  - definition: To make something become more active.
  - example: Exercise can stimulate the heart and increase blood flow.

- trigger
  - definition: To cause something to start suddenly.
  - example: Certain foods can trigger an allergic reaction.
