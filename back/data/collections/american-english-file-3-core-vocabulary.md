# American English File 3

## 1A Eating in and out

- salmon
  - definition: A large fish with pink meat that people eat.
  - example: We had grilled salmon for dinner.

- shrimp
  - definition: A small sea animal with a shell that people eat.
  - example: She ordered shrimp with rice.

- squid
  - definition: A sea animal with a soft body and long arms.
  - example: The restaurant serves fried squid.

- tuna
  - definition: A large sea fish that is often sold in cans.
  - example: I made a tuna sandwich for lunch.

- lamb
  - definition: Meat from a young sheep.
  - example: They cooked lamb with vegetables.

- avocado
  - definition: A green fruit with soft, creamy flesh and a large seed.
  - example: I put avocado in my salad.

- beet
  - definition: A dark red root vegetable.
  - example: Roasted beet tastes sweet.

- cabbage
  - definition: A round vegetable with many green or purple leaves.
  - example: She added cabbage to the soup.

- cucumber
  - definition: A long green vegetable that is often eaten raw.
  - example: We sliced cucumber for the salad.

- eggplant
  - definition: A large purple vegetable with soft flesh when cooked.
  - example: They grilled eggplant with olive oil.

- zucchini
  - definition: A long green vegetable similar to a cucumber, usually cooked.
  - example: I added zucchini to the pasta.

- baked
  - definition: Cooked in an oven with dry heat.
  - example: We had baked potatoes with dinner.

- boiled
  - definition: Cooked in very hot water.
  - example: He ate two boiled eggs.

- fried
  - definition: Cooked in hot oil or fat.
  - example: I try not to eat too much fried food.

- grilled
  - definition: Cooked over strong direct heat.
  - example: The grilled fish was delicious.

- roasted
  - definition: Cooked in an oven or over a fire until brown.
  - example: We made roasted vegetables.

- steamed
  - definition: Cooked using the hot steam from boiling water.
  - example: Steamed vegetables can stay crisp.

- ready-made food
  - definition: Food that is already prepared and only needs little or no cooking.
  - example: I bought ready-made food because I was tired.

- take-out food / takeout food
  - definition: Food bought from a restaurant to eat somewhere else.
  - example: We ordered take-out food for dinner.

- spicy
  - definition: Having a strong hot taste from spices such as chili.
  - example: This curry is too spicy for me.

- allergic
  - definition: Having a bad physical reaction to a particular food or substance.
  - example: She is allergic to peanuts.

- intolerant
  - definition: Unable to eat or use something without feeling ill.
  - example: He is intolerant to dairy products.

- cut down on
  - definition: To use, eat, or drink less of something.
  - example: I am trying to cut down on sugar.

- cut out
  - definition: To stop eating, drinking, or using something completely.
  - example: She cut out soda from her diet.

## 1B Modern families

- stepmother
  - definition: A woman who is married to your parent but is not your mother.
  - example: My stepmother joined us for dinner.

- brother-in-law
  - definition: The brother of your husband or wife, or the man married to your sibling.
  - example: My brother-in-law lives in Boston.

- stepsister
  - definition: A daughter of your stepparent who is not your biological sister.
  - example: I share a room with my stepsister.

- half-sister
  - definition: A sister who has one parent in common with you.
  - example: My half-sister is five years younger than me.

- adopted child
  - definition: A child who legally becomes part of a family different from the family they were born into.
  - example: They have one adopted child.

- only child
  - definition: A person who has no brothers or sisters.
  - example: He grew up as an only child.

- siblings
  - definition: Brothers and sisters.
  - example: I have three siblings.

- immediate family
  - definition: Your closest family, usually parents, children, brothers, sisters, and partner.
  - example: Only my immediate family came to the small wedding.

- extended family
  - definition: Family members beyond your closest family, such as grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins.
  - example: We see our extended family every summer.

- single-parent family
  - definition: A family in which one parent is mainly responsible for the children.
  - example: She grew up in a single-parent family.

- affectionate
  - definition: Showing love and care openly.
  - example: He is very affectionate with his children.

- ambitious
  - definition: Wanting very much to be successful.
  - example: She is ambitious and works hard.

- anxious
  - definition: Worried or nervous about something.
  - example: I felt anxious before the exam.

- bossy
  - definition: Always telling other people what to do.
  - example: My older brother can be bossy.

- charming
  - definition: Pleasant and attractive in a way that makes people like you.
  - example: Everyone thought the new teacher was charming.

- competitive
  - definition: Wanting to win or be better than other people.
  - example: She is very competitive when she plays tennis.

- imaginative
  - definition: Good at thinking of new and interesting ideas.
  - example: The children wrote imaginative stories.

- independent
  - definition: Able to do things and make decisions without much help from others.
  - example: He became more independent after moving out.

- insecure
  - definition: Not feeling confident about yourself or your situation.
  - example: She sometimes feels insecure about her English.

- mature
  - definition: Behaving in a sensible adult way.
  - example: He is very mature for his age.

- moody
  - definition: Often changing from happy to unhappy or angry.
  - example: He gets moody when he is tired.

- rebellious
  - definition: Not willing to follow rules or accept control.
  - example: She was quite rebellious as a teenager.

- reliable
  - definition: Someone you can trust to do what they say.
  - example: Sara is reliable and always arrives on time.

- self-confident
  - definition: Feeling sure about yourself and your abilities.
  - example: He sounds self-confident in interviews.

- sensible
  - definition: Showing good judgment and making practical decisions.
  - example: Taking an umbrella was a sensible idea.

- sensitive
  - definition: Easily affected by other people's feelings or by what happens.
  - example: She is sensitive to criticism.

- sociable
  - definition: Enjoying spending time and talking with other people.
  - example: He is sociable and makes friends easily.

- spoiled
  - definition: Used to getting everything you want, often because people give you too much.
  - example: The child became spoiled because nobody said no to him.

- stubborn
  - definition: Not willing to change your mind or behavior easily.
  - example: He is too stubborn to ask for help.

- sympathetic
  - definition: Showing that you understand and care about another person's problems.
  - example: My manager was sympathetic when I explained the problem.

## Practical English 1 — Meeting the parents

- You're kidding
  - definition: Used to show surprise because something is hard to believe.
  - example: You won the lottery? You're kidding!

- I don't believe it
  - definition: Used when you are very surprised by something.
  - example: I don't believe it—you got the job!

- How fantastic
  - definition: Used to react very positively to good or exciting news.
  - example: You are going to Paris? How fantastic!

- That's great news
  - definition: Used to say that you are happy about good news someone has told you.
  - example: You passed the exam? That's great news!

- What a pity
  - definition: Used to show that you are sorry about a disappointing situation.
  - example: You can't come with us? What a pity.

- Never mind
  - definition: Used to tell someone not to worry because something is not important.
  - example: You forgot the coffee? Never mind.

## 2A Spending money

- inherit
  - definition: To receive money or property from someone after they die.
  - example: Some people inherit money from their parents.

- save money
  - definition: To keep money instead of spending it so you can use it later.
  - example: I try to save money every month.

- lend
  - definition: To give someone money or something for a time and expect it back.
  - example: Can you lend me twenty dollars?

- borrow
  - definition: To take money or something for a time and give it back later.
  - example: Can I borrow your charger for an hour?

- waste money
  - definition: To spend money on something that is not useful or necessary.
  - example: Do not waste money on things you never use.

- afford
  - definition: To have enough money to pay for something.
  - example: I cannot afford a new car right now.

- charge
  - definition: To ask someone to pay a particular amount of money.
  - example: Some hotels charge extra for breakfast.

- owe
  - definition: To need to pay money back to someone.
  - example: I still owe my brother fifty dollars.

- invest
  - definition: To put money into something because you hope it will grow in value or make a profit.
  - example: They plan to invest some money in the new business.

- earn
  - definition: To get money for work that you do.
  - example: I need to earn enough money to pay the rent.

- be worth
  - definition: To have a particular value in money or importance.
  - example: A good education can be worth the time and effort.

- raise money
  - definition: To collect money for a person, group, or cause.
  - example: We want to raise money for a local charity.

- spend money on
  - definition: To use money to buy or pay for something.
  - example: I do not want to spend money on things I do not need.

- get into debt
  - definition: To reach a situation where you owe money.
  - example: It is easy to get into debt if you spend more than you earn.

- bill
  - definition: A written statement showing how much money you need to pay.
  - example: The electricity bill came this morning.

- budget
  - definition: A plan for how much money you can spend and what you will spend it on.
  - example: We made a monthly budget for food and rent.

- contactless payment
  - definition: A way to pay by touching or holding a card or device close to a payment machine.
  - example: I used contactless payment at the café.

- insurance
  - definition: Money you pay to a company so it can help cover the cost of certain problems or losses.
  - example: Car insurance can be expensive.

- loan
  - definition: Money that you borrow and must pay back, usually over time.
  - example: They took out a loan to buy the car.

- mortgage
  - definition: A long-term loan used to buy a house or apartment.
  - example: They are still paying their mortgage.

- salary
  - definition: The regular money a person is paid for their job, usually each month or year.
  - example: Her salary increased after the promotion.

- tax
  - definition: Money that people or businesses must pay to the government.
  - example: A part of my salary goes to tax.

- pay back
  - definition: To return money that you borrowed.
  - example: I promise to pay back the money next week.

- live on
  - definition: To use a particular amount of money for all your daily needs.
  - example: It is hard to live on a small salary.

- bargain
  - definition: Something sold for a lower price than you would normally expect.
  - example: These shoes were a real bargain.

## 3A Survive the drive

- freeway
  - definition: A wide road for fast traffic, usually with several lanes in each direction.
  - example: Traffic was heavy on the freeway.

- platform
  - definition: The raised area beside a train where passengers get on and off.
  - example: The train leaves from platform five.

- scooter
  - definition: A small two-wheeled vehicle, often with a motor.
  - example: He rented a scooter near the beach.

- subway
  - definition: An underground city train system.
  - example: The subway is the fastest way downtown.

- van
  - definition: A road vehicle larger than a car, used for carrying people or things.
  - example: We rented a van to move the furniture.

- bicycle lane
  - definition: A part of a road marked for bicycles only.
  - example: Please stay in the bicycle lane.

- car crash
  - definition: An accident in which a car hits another vehicle, person, or object.
  - example: The road was closed after a car crash.

- gas station
  - definition: A place where you can buy fuel for a vehicle.
  - example: We stopped at a gas station to fill the tank.

- parking ticket
  - definition: A notice requiring you to pay money because you parked illegally.
  - example: I got a parking ticket downtown.

- pedestrian zone
  - definition: An area where cars are not allowed and people can walk safely.
  - example: The old center is a pedestrian zone.

- rush hour
  - definition: The busy time when many people travel to or from work.
  - example: The roads are crowded during rush hour.

- speed camera
  - definition: A camera that records vehicles that are driving too fast.
  - example: There is a speed camera near the school.

- speed limit
  - definition: The fastest speed that the law allows on a road.
  - example: The speed limit here is 50 miles per hour.

- traffic jam
  - definition: A long line of vehicles that are moving very slowly or not moving.
  - example: We were late because of a traffic jam.

- seat belt
  - definition: A safety belt that holds you in your seat in a vehicle.
  - example: Always wear your seat belt.

- set off
  - definition: To start a journey.
  - example: We set off early to avoid traffic.

- pick up
  - definition: To go somewhere to collect a person and take them with you.
  - example: I can pick up my sister from the station.

- drop off
  - definition: To take someone somewhere and leave them there.
  - example: Can you drop off this package on your way to work?

- end up
  - definition: To finally be in a place or situation, often without planning it.
  - example: If we take the wrong road, we may end up far from the city.

- run out of gas
  - definition: To use all the fuel in a vehicle so none is left.
  - example: Always check the fuel so you do not run out of gas.

- get stuck
  - definition: To become unable to move or continue.
  - example: We often get stuck in traffic during rush hour.

- get around
  - definition: To travel from place to place in an area.
  - example: The subway makes it easy to get around the city.

- public transportation
  - definition: Buses, trains, and other transport that anyone can use.
  - example: I usually take public transportation to work.

- directions
  - definition: Instructions that tell you how to get to a place.
  - example: Can you give me directions to the hotel?
