# Cambridge Vocabulary for IELTS Advanced

## Unit 1 — Human nature

- adolescent
  - definition: A young person who is developing from a child into an adult.
  - example: The program gives support to each adolescent during this difficult stage.

- characteristic
  - definition: A typical quality or feature of a person or thing.
  - example: Patience is an important characteristic of a good teacher.

- trait
  - definition: A particular quality in someone's character.
  - example: Curiosity is a useful trait for a scientist.

- apprehensive
  - definition: Worried because you think something unpleasant may happen.
  - example: She felt apprehensive before starting her new job.

- assertive
  - definition: Confident about saying what you want or believe without being rude.
  - example: He was assertive when he explained his needs.

- clumsy
  - definition: Moving or doing things in an awkward way and often causing accidents.
  - example: I am clumsy and often drop things.

- cynical
  - definition: Believing that people usually act for selfish reasons.
  - example: She was cynical about the politician's promises.

- desirable
  - definition: Worth having or wanting because it is useful or attractive.
  - example: Good communication skills are desirable in this job.

- eccentric
  - definition: Strange or unusual in a way that is different from most people.
  - example: The eccentric professor wore a bright hat every day.

- egotistical
  - definition: Thinking and talking too much about how important or good you are.
  - example: His egotistical comments annoyed the rest of the team.

- gullible
  - definition: Too ready to believe things that may not be true.
  - example: Gullible people can easily fall for online scams.

- idealised / idealized
  - definition: Shown or thought of as better and more perfect than it really is.
  - example: The film gives an idealised picture of village life.

- inconsiderate
  - definition: Not thinking about how your actions affect other people.
  - example: It was inconsiderate to play loud music at midnight.

- indecisive
  - definition: Unable to make decisions easily.
  - example: He is indecisive and takes a long time to choose.

- self-assured
  - definition: Calm and confident about yourself and your abilities.
  - example: She sounded self-assured during the interview.

- self-absorbed
  - definition: Thinking mainly about yourself and your own problems or interests.
  - example: He seemed self-absorbed and never asked about anyone else.

- self-centred / self-centered
  - definition: Caring mainly about yourself rather than other people.
  - example: The character is selfish and self-centred at the start of the story.

- self-congratulatory
  - definition: Showing too much pleasure and pride in your own success.
  - example: The speech sounded self-congratulatory after the small victory.

- self-deprecating
  - definition: Making yourself seem less important or less good, often in a humorous way.
  - example: His self-deprecating jokes made the audience relax.

- self-important
  - definition: Acting as if you are more important than other people.
  - example: The self-important manager expected special treatment.

- tactful
  - definition: Careful not to say or do something that may upset someone.
  - example: She gave a tactful answer to the difficult question.

- well-adjusted
  - definition: Emotionally healthy and able to deal well with normal life.
  - example: The children seem happy and well-adjusted.

- well-bred
  - definition: Having polite manners because of a good social upbringing.
  - example: He was quiet, polite, and well-bred.

- well-brought-up
  - definition: Taught to behave politely and consider other people.
  - example: The children were well-brought-up and thanked their hosts.

- well-dressed
  - definition: Wearing attractive and suitable clothes.
  - example: A well-dressed man was waiting in the lobby.

## Unit 2 — Time for a change

- era
  - definition: A long period of history with a particular character or style.
  - example: The internet created a new era in communication.

- evolution
  - definition: A gradual process of change and development over time.
  - example: The museum shows the evolution of transport over two centuries.

- finds
  - definition: Objects or remains discovered by people such as archaeologists.
  - example: The latest finds include tools and pieces of pottery.

- pioneer
  - definition: A person who is among the first to develop or use a new idea.
  - example: She was a pioneer in modern medical research.

- remnants
  - definition: Small parts that remain after most of something has disappeared.
  - example: Archaeologists found remnants of an ancient wall.

- retrospect
  - definition: A way of looking back at past events.
  - example: In retrospect, changing jobs was the right decision.

- status quo
  - definition: The situation that exists now, especially when people want it to stay the same.
  - example: Some leaders prefer to protect the status quo.

- transition
  - definition: The process of changing from one state or situation to another.
  - example: The country is going through a transition to cleaner energy.

- trend
  - definition: A general direction in which something is changing or developing.
  - example: The data shows a trend toward online shopping.

- abrupt
  - definition: Sudden and unexpected.
  - example: There was an abrupt change in the weather.

- bygone
  - definition: Belonging to a time in the past.
  - example: The photographs show a bygone way of life.

- fleeting
  - definition: Lasting for only a very short time.
  - example: She caught a fleeting view of the bird.

- immense
  - definition: Extremely large or great.
  - example: The project required an immense amount of work.

- infinitesimal
  - definition: Extremely small.
  - example: The chance of error was infinitesimal.

- moderate
  - definition: Neither very large nor very small in amount or degree.
  - example: Prices rose at a moderate rate.

- periodic
  - definition: Happening again from time to time.
  - example: The machine needs periodic checks.

- preceding
  - definition: Coming before something else in time or order.
  - example: Sales were higher than in the preceding year.

- prehistoric
  - definition: Belonging to the time before written history.
  - example: The cave contains prehistoric drawings.

- profound
  - definition: Very great or important in effect, meaning, or feeling.
  - example: The invention had a profound effect on society.

- rapid
  - definition: Happening very quickly.
  - example: The city experienced rapid growth.

- topical
  - definition: Related to a subject that is important or discussed now.
  - example: Climate change remains a topical issue.

- tremendous
  - definition: Extremely large, strong, or impressive.
  - example: The new bridge has brought tremendous benefits.

- turbulent
  - definition: Full of sudden change, trouble, or uncertainty.
  - example: The country went through a turbulent period.

- pressed for time
  - definition: Having very little time to do what you need to do.
  - example: I was pressed for time, so I skipped lunch.

- have the time of your life
  - definition: To enjoy yourself more than you usually do.
  - example: You can have the time of your life on a trip with close friends.

- have time on your hands
  - definition: To have more free time than you need.
  - example: After the exams, you may have time on your hands.

- in next to no time
  - definition: Very quickly.
  - example: The new train gets us there in next to no time.

- in the blink of an eye
  - definition: Extremely quickly, in a moment.
  - example: The summer seemed to pass in the blink of an eye.

- there aren't enough hours in the day
  - definition: Used to say you have too much to do and too little time.
  - example: With work and study, there aren't enough hours in the day.

- time goes so fast
  - definition: Used to say that a period seems to pass very quickly.
  - example: When I am with old friends, time goes so fast.

- excavate
  - definition: To carefully remove earth to find things buried below it.
  - example: Archaeologists began to excavate the site in spring.

## Unit 3 — No man is an island

- behaviour / behavior
  - definition: The way a person or animal acts.
  - example: The school expects respectful behaviour from all students.

- demographics
  - definition: Information about groups of people, such as their age, income, or location.
  - example: The company studies customer demographics before opening a store.

- heritage
  - definition: The traditions, history, and culture passed down from earlier generations.
  - example: The festival celebrates the region's cultural heritage.

- interaction
  - definition: Communication or activity between people or things.
  - example: Online classes still need regular interaction between students.

- minority
  - definition: A smaller group within a larger population.
  - example: The policy aims to protect the rights of the minority.

- norm
  - definition: A usual or accepted way of behaving or doing something.
  - example: Working from home has become the norm in some industries.

- peer
  - definition: A person of about the same age, position, or level as another person.
  - example: Teenagers are often influenced by a peer.

- pressure
  - definition: Strong influence that tries to make someone do something.
  - example: She felt pressure to follow the group.

- segment
  - definition: One part of a larger group or market.
  - example: The company targets a young segment of the population.

- standard
  - definition: A level of quality or behaviour that is considered acceptable.
  - example: The hospital follows a high standard of care.

- status
  - definition: A person's social position or level of importance.
  - example: The job gave him higher status in the community.

- anti-social / antisocial
  - definition: Avoiding other people or behaving in a way that harms social life.
  - example: His anti-social behaviour made group work difficult.

- conservative
  - definition: Preferring traditional ideas and being cautious about change.
  - example: The town has a conservative attitude toward new developments.

- conventional
  - definition: Following the usual or traditional way of doing things.
  - example: They chose a conventional wedding ceremony.

- harmonious
  - definition: Friendly and peaceful, with people or parts working well together.
  - example: The two communities have a harmonious relationship.

- influential
  - definition: Having the power to affect people's ideas or actions.
  - example: She is an influential voice in education.

- multicultural
  - definition: Including people and cultures from many different backgrounds.
  - example: Toronto is a multicultural city.

- suburban
  - definition: Related to an area outside the centre of a city where many people live.
  - example: They moved to a suburban neighbourhood.

- unconventional
  - definition: Different from what is usual or traditional.
  - example: He took an unconventional approach to the problem.

- follow accepted behaviour
  - definition: To act in the way a group or society considers normal.
  - example: New employees are expected to follow accepted behaviour in the office.

- shun mainstream values
  - definition: To deliberately avoid the common beliefs and habits of most people.
  - example: Some groups shun mainstream values and choose a very different lifestyle.

- conform
  - definition: To behave according to accepted rules or expectations.
  - example: Young people may feel pressure to conform.

- exclude
  - definition: To keep someone or something out of a group or activity.
  - example: The club should not exclude people because of age.

- flaunt
  - definition: To show something proudly so that other people notice it.
  - example: He likes to flaunt his expensive watch.

- obey
  - definition: To do what a rule, law, or person in authority says.
  - example: Drivers must obey the speed limit.

- skew
  - definition: To make information or results unfairly different from the true picture.
  - example: A small sample can skew the results.

## Unit 4 — Scientific discovery

- capsule
  - definition: A small case containing medicine that you swallow.
  - example: Take one capsule after breakfast.

- carbon dioxide
  - definition: A gas produced when people and animals breathe out and when fuels burn.
  - example: Burning coal releases carbon dioxide into the air.

- compound
  - definition: A substance made from two or more chemical elements joined together.
  - example: Water is a compound of hydrogen and oxygen.

- consequences
  - definition: The results or effects of an action or event.
  - example: We need to think about the consequences of using this chemical.

- dose
  - definition: A measured amount of medicine taken at one time.
  - example: The doctor reduced the dose.

- emissions
  - definition: Gases or other substances released into the air.
  - example: New rules aim to cut vehicle emissions.

- exposure
  - definition: The state of coming into contact with something, especially something harmful.
  - example: Long exposure to strong sunlight can damage skin.

- food chain
  - definition: A series showing how living things depend on each other for food.
  - example: Pollution can move through the food chain.

- infection
  - definition: An illness caused by harmful bacteria, viruses, or other organisms entering the body.
  - example: The wound developed an infection.

- pesticide
  - definition: A chemical used to kill insects or other organisms that damage crops.
  - example: Farmers reduced their use of pesticide near the river.

- protein
  - definition: A substance in food that the body needs for growth and repair.
  - example: Beans are a good source of protein.

- reaction
  - definition: A chemical change that happens when substances affect each other.
  - example: Mixing the two liquids caused a reaction.

- side effects
  - definition: Unwanted effects of a medicine or treatment.
  - example: The drug may cause mild side effects.

- bacterial
  - definition: Caused by or related to bacteria.
  - example: The doctor said it was a bacterial infection.

- contagious
  - definition: Able to spread from one person or animal to another.
  - example: The illness is highly contagious.

- contaminated
  - definition: Made unsafe or dirty by harmful substances.
  - example: The water was contaminated by waste from the factory.

- controversial
  - definition: Causing strong disagreement among people.
  - example: The new treatment is still controversial.

- crucial
  - definition: Extremely important.
  - example: Clean water is crucial for public health.

- essential
  - definition: Completely necessary.
  - example: Regular sleep is essential for good health.

- natural
  - definition: Existing in nature rather than made by people.
  - example: The product contains only natural ingredients.

- organic
  - definition: Related to living things or natural substances rather than being artificially made.
  - example: Researchers found organic compounds in the soil.

- petrochemicals
  - definition: Chemicals made from oil or natural gas and used to make many products.
  - example: Plastics are often produced from petrochemicals.

- absorb
  - definition: To take in a liquid, gas, heat, or other substance.
  - example: Plants absorb water through their roots.

- contaminate
  - definition: To make something dirty or unsafe by adding a harmful substance.
  - example: Waste from the mine could contaminate the river.

- release
  - definition: To allow a substance to escape or enter the environment.
  - example: The factory must not release toxic gases.

- establishment

- formulation

- inconsistency

- insignificance

- interpretation

- technician

- theorist

- vocation

- analytical

- blue-collar

- conceptual

- indicative

- a process of trial and error

- give yourself something to aim for
  - definition: To set a clear target that helps you stay motivated.

- make a concerted effort
  - definition: To try very hard in an organised and determined way.

- set yourself a goal
  - definition: To decide on something specific that you want to achieve.
  - example: Before the course, set yourself a goal.

  - definition: Impossible to prevent or escape.

- assure

- tempt
  - definition: To make someone want to do or have something.

- urge
