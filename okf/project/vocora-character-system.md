Vocora uses a recurring cast to make language practice coherent, recognizable,
and emotionally engaging. Characters are part of exercises, stories, role-play,
feedback, and rewards rather than decorative illustrations added after the
learning experience is designed.

The system borrows the durable product lesson behind Duolingo's cast—a familiar,
distinctive group can support motivation, voice variety, and compact
storytelling—but it must not copy Duolingo's characters or visual language.
Vocora's differentiator is connected progression from exposure to mastery across
vocabulary, listening, pronunciation, spelling, grammar, reading, writing, and
speaking.

# World and Cast

The setting is a **Language Studio**, not a school, classroom, or cartoon city.
Its characters are contemporary people with work, travel, study, relationships,
mistakes, and goals. Language connects their lives and supplies believable
contexts for practice.

V1 has six recurring human characters—Mira, Noa, Aya, Kian, Rafi, and Tala—plus
one small abstract companion. A character represents a way of using language,
not ownership of a single skill. Each character has a primary learning bias but
can appear throughout the learning path, including in situations that expose the
character's own limitations.

This is **skill affinity, not skill ownership**. For example, Mira can appear in
listening or make an error in writing; Aya can speak; and Kian can participate in
a real-world exchange where Tala challenges an overgeneralized rule.

# Learning Behavior

Characters respond in character while still serving the learning objective.
Their feedback must be specific, non-humiliating, and honest about the learner's
evidence.

## Mira

Mira helps learners who know language but hesitate to speak. On an incorrect
response she protects conversational momentum and invites another attempt before
polishing accuracy. Correct responses can receive a quick, physical celebration.

## Noa

Noa identifies the exact point of an error instead of returning a generic wrong
state. She never humiliates the learner or silently ignores a mistake. Her
celebration is restrained—a small smile or check—so success does not make her
behave like Mira.

## Aya

Aya draws attention to what was actually heard: stress, sound, rhythm, or the
problematic part of an utterance. An error can trigger a small listening gesture
and a targeted replay. Correct feedback remains subtle rather than becoming a
large celebration.

## Kian

Kian first exposes the relevant pattern and lets the learner reason from it. He
does not default to a detached grammar lecture. His tendency to over-analyze is a
story and dialogue opportunity, not permission to make instruction unnecessarily
complex.

## Rafi

Rafi places words in context through stories, collocations, related concepts,
and other cast members. His warmth supports vocabulary and reading, while his
tendency to continue too long gives other characters a natural reason to
interrupt or redirect him.

## Tala

Tala distinguishes grammatical possibility from pragmatic fit. She shows how
audience, relationship, setting, formality, and intention affect choices such as
`Can you...?`, `Could you...?`, and `Would you mind...?`. She must remain a
specific modern person, not a generic "wise grandmother" stereotype.

# Relationship Engine

Relationships are part of the system because familiar tensions create reusable
story and role-play situations without introducing a new cast for every lesson.

* **Mira and Noa** embody the productive tension between fluency and accuracy.
* **Aya and Rafi** contrast concentrated listening with expansive storytelling.
* **Kian and Tala** contrast clean rules with context-sensitive real usage.

These pairings are not rival teams or fixed lesson assignments. A story can
combine different characters whenever their goals, strengths, flaws, and
relationships produce a natural language situation.

# Companion Direction

Vocora's companion is an abstract visual-feedback presence derived from voice,
sound, and language—not an owl, other animal, human teacher, or skill owner.

The current working concept is a small speech-bubble and waveform form with the
temporary name **Echo**. The name is not final. The companion can become wavy
during listening, pulse during speaking, lose coherence after an error, and
resolve into a complete form when mastery evidence is achieved.

The companion supports orientation, feedback, and progress visibility. It must
not replace instruction, take credit for the learner's achievement, use guilt to
force retention, or celebrate completion as if it proved mastery.

# Mastery-Aware Feedback

Characters react to the learner's actual evidence, not only to session activity.
A completed lesson with weak listening evidence must not receive a false full
mastery celebration. Feedback may acknowledge completion while keeping the gap
explicit, for example: "Lesson finished. Aya still has listening work for you."

This creates three durable rules:

1. Completion, progress, and mastery remain distinct states.
2. Character reactions cannot contradict the learner's recorded evidence.
3. Motivation must support learning truth rather than disguise unfinished work.

# Visual and Voice Language

Vocora's cast is stylized but more adult in proportion than a chibi system.
Faces remain simple, with eyes approximately `30-40%` smaller than the
Duolingo-inspired comparison in the source brief. Rounded geometry must not
imitate Duolingo's signature character construction.

* Every character needs a recognizable silhouette at approximately `48-64px`.
* One primary accent color identifies each character; shared brand tokens remain
  the source for final palette values.
* A shared rhythm curve inspired by a waveform can recur in hair, collars,
  clothing, or silhouettes to make the cast feel like one world.
* Each character needs an individual movement vocabulary. Do not apply one
  animation to six visual skins.
* Voice direction includes pace, energy, intonation, pausing, vocabulary style,
  and delivery—not merely a different synthetic voice.
* Voice variety should expose learners to meaningfully different speaking
  patterns while keeping pronunciation reliable for the lesson context.

# Character Bible Contract

Before final illustration, animation, synthetic voice, role-play, or generative
dialogue work, each character needs a reviewed Character Bible containing:

* appearance and silhouette;
* expressions, gestures, and movement vocabulary;
* voice, pace, intonation, and vocabulary style;
* personality, backstory, goals, strengths, and flaws;
* relationships and recurring tensions;
* correct, incorrect, retry, completion, and mastery reactions;
* behavior in exercises, stories, and role-play;
* explicit out-of-character behaviors the character must never perform.

Runtime and AI-authored experiences must consume the same stable character
identity. A character must not change personality merely because the exercise,
reward state, or content-generation mechanism changes.

# Design Invariants

* Characters participate in learning; they are not decorative wrappers.
* Characters are believable people with strengths and flaws, not personified
  skill buttons.
* Skill affinity never becomes exclusive skill ownership.
* Personality, feedback, voice, and movement remain consistent across surfaces.
* Diversity should cover age, background, voice, and temperament without using
  a character as a stereotype or token.
* Familiar relationships should reduce storytelling setup and generate natural
  communication situations.
* Visual simplicity serves recognition and animation, not imitation of another
  product's shapes or mascot formula.
* All encouragement remains compatible with truthful mastery evidence.
