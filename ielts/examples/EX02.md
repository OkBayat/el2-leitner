# EX02 — Shopping: quantities and the final order

Original Vocora-commissioned fictional teaching material; not an IELTS test or a certified band exemplar.

Associated design lesson: **L0081**. Status: **authored reference pack, not a complete runtime lesson**.

## Target lexicon

| Target | Role | Meaning | Original example |

|---|---|---|---|

| ingredient | count noun | one of the foods used to make a dish | Rice is one ingredient in this meal. |

| vegetable | count noun | a plant or part of a plant eaten as food | We buy fresh vegetables. |

| carrot | count noun | a long orange vegetable | We need two carrots. |

| onion | count noun | a vegetable with layers and a strong taste | Chop one onion. |

| pepper | count noun in this context | a red, green or yellow vegetable | Add a red pepper. |

| cheese | normally uncountable noun | a food made from milk | We need some cheese. |



**Word partners:** a kilo of rice; a bottle of oil; pay in cash


## Reading stimulus

The neighbourhood cooking club meets on Saturday. This week, six people are making a vegetable meal. Leila has a shopping list. She needs one kilo of rice, four carrots, two onions and one red pepper. There is already some oil in the club kitchen, so she does not need to buy a bottle. The club also has salt. Leila wants some cheese for a small salad, but the shop does not have any today. She decides to make the salad without it. The vegetables cost eight pounds, and the rice costs three pounds. Leila pays eleven pounds in cash. She keeps the receipt. The club starts cooking at ten, but Leila arrives at half past nine to wash the vegetables. The club is next to the library. The notice does not give the library's opening time.


## Listening script — audio not yet recorded

Shopkeeper: Good morning. What would you like?
Customer: Two kilos of rice, please. Sorry, just one kilo. There are only three of us today.
Shopkeeper: One kilo. Anything else?
Customer: Three onions and a bottle of oil.
Shopkeeper: The small bottle or the large one?
Customer: The small one, please. Do you have any carrots?
Shopkeeper: Yes. They are two pounds a kilo.
Customer: I'll take one kilo of carrots as well.
Shopkeeper: That is nine pounds in total.
Customer: Can I pay by card?
Shopkeeper: I'm sorry, the machine is not working.
Customer: That's all right. I'll pay in cash. Could I have a receipt?
Shopkeeper: Of course. Here you are.


## Reading questions and answer rationales

### EX02-R01 · `short-answer`

How many people are cooking on Saturday? ONE WORD OR A NUMBER.

**Answer:** six / 6

**Why:** The second sentence gives six.

### EX02-R02 · `short-answer`

How many onions are on Leila's list? ONE WORD OR A NUMBER.

**Answer:** two / 2

**Why:** The list says two onions.

### EX02-R03 · `truth`

Leila buys some oil. TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN?

**Answer:** FALSE

**Why:** Oil is already available, so she does not buy it.

### EX02-R04 · `truth`

The shop has cheese today. TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN?

**Answer:** FALSE

**Why:** It does not have any today.

### EX02-R05 · `short-answer`

What is the total cost in pounds? ONE WORD OR A NUMBER.

**Answer:** eleven / 11

**Why:** Eight for vegetables plus three for rice equals eleven.

### EX02-R06 · `short-answer`

At what time does Leila arrive? Write a time.

**Answer:** 9:30 / half past nine

**Why:** Arrival is earlier than the ten o'clock cooking start.

### EX02-R07 · `truth`

The library opens at nine. TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN?

**Answer:** NOT GIVEN

**Why:** The text explicitly says the opening time is not given.

### EX02-R08 · `error-correction`

Correct only the quantity question: How many rice does Leila buy?

**Answer:** How much rice does Leila buy?

**Why:** Rice is uncountable here; kilos would be countable.


## Listening questions and answer rationales

### EX02-A01 · `short-answer`

Final rice order, in kilos: ONE WORD OR A NUMBER.

**Answer:** one / 1

**Why:** Two is explicitly corrected to one.

### EX02-A02 · `short-answer`

Number of onions: ONE WORD OR A NUMBER.

**Answer:** three / 3

**Why:** The customer orders three onions.

### EX02-A03 · `short-answer`

Oil bottle size: ONE WORD.

**Answer:** small

**Why:** The customer chooses the small bottle.

### EX02-A04 · `short-answer`

Carrots cost ___ pounds a kilo. ONE WORD OR A NUMBER.

**Answer:** two / 2

**Why:** This is the unit price, not the total purchase price.

### EX02-A05 · `short-answer`

Total cost: ___ pounds. ONE WORD OR A NUMBER.

**Answer:** nine / 9

**Why:** The shopkeeper states the total.

### EX02-A06 · `choice`

How does the customer finally pay: card, cash, or online transfer?

**Answer:** cash

**Why:** Card is requested but unavailable; cash is the final method.

### EX02-A07 · `short-answer`

What document does the customer request? ONE WORD.

**Answer:** receipt

**Why:** Could I have a receipt? provides the exact word.

### EX02-A08 · `choice`

Why is the rice order reduced: fewer people, a higher price, or no rice in stock?

**Answer:** fewer people

**Why:** The customer says there are only three people today.


## Speaking

Role-play a shop order. Ask for two ingredients, specify a quantity, then politely correct one part of your order. Answer a follow-up about payment.


## Writing

Write 40–65 words explaining what you need for a simple meal and what you already have. Use some/any and one countable quantity.


### Original model — show after submission

We are making a vegetable meal for three people. We need one kilo of rice, two onions and some carrots. We already have some oil, so we do not need another bottle. There is not any cheese in the kitchen, but we can make the meal without it.


## Design and assessment note

The teaching passage and listening order use different people, quantities and final decisions. For a test, hide the script and do not reveal the corrected order beforehand. A synthetic dialogue is acceptable for formative rehearsal only; a readiness task needs natural, quality-controlled audio.
